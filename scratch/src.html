Comprehensive Overview

What are All on 4 Dental Implants (full arch)?

All on 4 is a full-arch treatment in which a complete fixed bridge of replacement teeth is supported by a small number of dental implants, most commonly four, and up to six when your anatomy calls for it. It is designed for patients missing all or most of their upper or lower teeth, and it removes the need to place an individual implant for every missing tooth.

The two rear implants may or may not be placed at an angle of up to 45 degrees. This is not just a technical detail. Sometimes, angling the rear implants lets them reach denser bone further forward in the jaw, which is why most patients can avoid the bone grafting traditional full-arch implant approaches often require. Less surgery, less recovery time, and, in most cases, a same-day temporary set of teeth, so you never go without a smile while things heal.

Same-day teeth also depend on adequate implant stability. [1–3]

The final bridge is fixed permanently to the implants. It does not come out at night. It does not need adhesive. It functions much more like your natural teeth than any removable denture can.

The bridge stays in place for daily use and is removed by a clinician when needed. It still requires daily cleaning and professional maintenance.

All on 4 vs All on X: What Do These Names Actually Mean?

If you've been researching fixed teeth, you've probably seen both “All on 4” and “All on X” and wondered whether they are different treatments. They're not. They are two names for the same thing: a full arch of fixed teeth supported by dental implants, so you never take them out.

“All on 4” is simply the best-known name for this treatment. It began as a treatment concept where a full arch is supported on four implants. “All on X” is the umbrella term the profession uses, where “X” stands for the number of implants, because the right number varies for every patient. How we decide that number, and why it never changes your price, is explained further down this page.

All on 4® is a registered trademark and treatment concept of Nobel Biocare. Smile Concepts is an independent practice and plans each full arch case using the implant system best suited to your case, which may include Neoss, DIO, Nobel Biocare, Straumann, NeoDent, Southern Implants or other leading implant systems.

All on 4 Surgery vs Individual Implants

Individual implants usually replace one or several teeth with crowns or smaller bridges. All on 4 supports an entire arch with one fixed bridge.

If you only have a few missing teeth and your remaining teeth are healthy, individual implants or another tooth-preserving option may be more appropriate.

Neither approach is automatically cheaper or better; the plan depends on bone requirements, restorative design, and the number of teeth being replaced.

Do I need All on 4 on both upper and lower Jaws?

An arch means the upper or lower set of teeth. One arch can be treated independently.

You may not need both arches to be treated. That is why a detailed assessment is required to make that decision. Your remaining teeth, opposing bite and oral health guide the plan.

Pricing is quoted per arch.

How many implants will I need? All on 2, All on 4, All on 6?

The number of implants chosen is based on the type of teeth you have selected as the end product (your final teeth).

If you want fixed bridges (fixed teeth that you cannot remove), then we place between 4 and 8 implants. If, however, you choose removable teeth as the final option, then 2 to 4 implants are a better choice.

Your dentist selects the number of implants after an examination and imaging, rather than fixing it in advance. More implants are not automatically better; their position, bone support and bridge design also matter.

Many patients are treated with four implants, the classic “all on 4”. Others do better with six or eight dental implants, especially where the bone is softer, or the bite is heavier. That is “all on 6” or “all on 8”. Same treatment, same fixed teeth, just more support where your anatomy calls for it.

Here is what matters most: we don't charge per implant. After thoroughly analysing your X-rays and 3D CBCT scan, your dentist decides whether four, six, or eight implants will give you the strongest, longest-lasting result, and the price per arch stays the same either way. Your treatment is driven by your anatomy, not by a price list.

All on 2 implants and sometimes all on 4 implants may be used for supporting removable dentures.

An implant-supported overdenture is removed for cleaning and may be an alternative to a fixed bridge. Gum disease should be assessed and managed before either option; a removable design does not eliminate the risk of disease around implants.

Recovery, Aftercare & Ongoing Maintenance after All on 4 Implant Surgery

Recovery from All on 4 is usually easier than people expect. Most Smile Concepts patients return to their normal routine within a few days, while the implants fuse with the jawbone over the next three to six months. Here is what to expect at each stage.

The First Few Days After Surgery: What to Expect

Some swelling, mild bruising, and general tenderness in the days after surgery are all normal. These effects are temporary and usually start fading within the first week. We'll go through your aftercare plan with you in detail, covering:

  • How to manage swelling and discomfort
  • What medication you'll need and how to take it
  • Oral hygiene during the early healing period
  • What you can eat, and what to avoid for now
  • When you can get back to normal activity

Eating During Healing

During this period, the implants fuse with the jawbone, so we recommend a softer diet to avoid putting unnecessary pressure on them. We'll explain exactly what's appropriate to eat at each stage of recovery.

Long-Term Maintenance

All on 4 is designed as a long-term solution, but it still needs ongoing care to stay that way. To help maintain the health of your implants and the surrounding tissue, we recommend:

  • Daily brushing and cleaning around the bridge
  • Following the oral hygiene instructions your clinician gives you
  • Attending regular dental check-ups
  • Booking in for professional implant maintenance and cleaning
  • Letting us know promptly if anything feels off

With proper care, many patients go on to enjoy their All on 4 implants for years to come.

Is There A Cheaper Alternative to All on 4?

Yes. If fixed All on 4 teeth are outside your budget, Smile Concepts offers snap on implant dentures, also called an implant overdenture or All on 2, from $12,000 per arch. It is a removable denture that clips onto implants, giving far more stability than a conventional denture at a lower cost than fixed teeth.

What Are Snap-on Implant Dentures or Clip-on Implant Dentures (All On 2)?

Unlike a conventional denture that rests on your gums and relies on suction or adhesive, a snap on implant denture is a custom made removable denture that clips securely onto dental implants placed in your jaw. In the lower jaw this usually means two implants, which is why it is often called All on 2. The upper jaw usually needs four implants because the bone is softer.

Much more secure: Special locator attachments hold the denture in place while you eat, laugh and speak, greatly reducing the slipping, rubbing and sore spots common with conventional dentures.

Removable for easy cleaning: You unclip it yourself for daily cleaning and at night.

Less palate coverage: In the upper jaw, implant support often allows a slimmer denture that leaves more of the roof of your mouth uncovered, so food tastes better and the denture feels less bulky.

A possible stepping stone: Depending on your bone and implant positions, an implant denture can sometimes be upgraded to fixed All on 4 teeth later. Your dentist will tell you whether your implants could be reused.

Worth knowing: The small clip inserts wear over time and are replaced periodically as part of routine maintenance.

Early Super Release

Can I Use My Superannuation to Pay for All on 4 Treatment?

Possibly. If dental treatment is needed to relieve chronic pain or a condition affecting your quality of life, you may be eligible to apply to the ATO for early release of superannuation on compassionate grounds.

The ATO assesses applications case by case against strict criteria, requires supporting reports from two practitioners and an itemised treatment plan, and recommends considering other payment options first. Consider the tax and retirement savings implications and seek independent financial advice. [7]

We work with SuperCare to help patients through this process. Ask us about it at your consultation or learn more about SuperCare here.

What You Should Know About Risks Associated with All on 4 Dental Implants

All on 4 (All on X) has a strong clinical track record, but it is a surgical procedure, and every patient should understand the risks before proceeding. Any surgical or invasive procedure carries risks. Before proceeding, you should seek a second opinion from an appropriately qualified health practitioner.

Healing varies: Overall health, blood supply, and adherence to post-operative instructions affect how well and how quickly implants integrate.
Smoking is associated with higher implant failure risk and greater bone loss. Discuss stopping smoking and individual risk management with your treating team. [4]
Medical history matters: Certain medications and conditions can affect healing, so we take a full history before proceeding.
Maintenance is not optional: The bridge and implants need regular professional cleaning. Skipping maintenance appointments increases the risk of complications over time.
Implant failure is possible: An implant may not integrate or may lose support later. Biological and mechanical complications can occur even after initial healing. [2–5]
