## What are All on 4 dental implants (full arch) ?

What are All on 4 dental implants (full arch) ? All on 4 is a full arch treatment where a complete fixed bridge of replacement teeth is supported by a small number of dental implants, most commonly four, and up to six where your anatomy calls for it. It's designed for patients missing all or most of their upper or lower teeth, and it removes the need to place an individual implant for every missing tooth. Some patients refer to this as All on 4 implants (full arch) treatment, while others simply call it All on four implants. The two rear implants are placed at an angle of up to 45 degrees. This is not just a technical detail. Angling the rear implants lets them reach denser areas of existing bone further forward in the jaw, which is why most patients can avoid the bone grafting that traditional full-arch implant approaches often require. Less surgery, less recovery time, and in most cases, a same-day temporary set of teeth, so you are never without a smile while things heal. The final bridge is fixed permanently to the implants. It does not come out at night. It does not need adhesive. It functions much more like your natural teeth than any removable denture can. Complete my smile Back to top

## All on 4 vs All on X: What Do These Names Mean?

All on 4 vs All on X: What Do These Names Mean? If you've been researching fixed teeth, you've probably seen both "All on 4" and "All on X" and wondered whether they're different treatments. They're not; they're two names for the same thing: a full arch of fixed teeth supported by dental implants, so you never take them out. "All on 4" is simply the best-known name for this treatment. It began as a concept where a full arch is supported on four implants. "All on X" is the umbrella term the profession uses, where the "X" stands for the number of implants, because the right number is different for every patient. Whether you're searching for All on X dental implants or the more familiar "All on 4," you'll find that both treatments are based on the same principle: replacing a full arch of teeth with a fixed implant-supported prosthesis. Today, many clinicians prefer the term All on X because it offers greater flexibility, using four, five, six, or more implants depending on what's clinically appropriate for each patient.
